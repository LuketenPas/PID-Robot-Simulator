PID Robot Simulator

What is PID?

PID stands for Proportional, Integral, and Derivative. It moves a system toward a target and helps it stay there.

  • P: Reacts to how far the robot is from the target.
  • I: Reacts to error that builds up over time.
  • D: Reacts to how quickly the error is changing.

What is PID tuning?

PID tuning is adjusting kP, kI, and kD to get the response you want. More kP usually reacts faster, kI fixes small leftover error, and kD helps calm overshoot and shaking.

Good tuning reaches the target quickly and settles smoothly. Bad tuning can be slow, overshoot the target, or oscillate back and forth.

What is PID used for?

PID control is used for robot driving, flywheels, arms, elevators, balancing, thermostats, cruise control, drones, and many other automatic systems.

How to use this simulator

  • Click anywhere on the field to choose a new target.
  • The square yellow FRC robot drives toward the target.
  • The four wheels use a symmetric swerve-style pattern.
  • The orange circle is the target position.
  • Use the sliders or number boxes to tune kP, kI, and kD.
  • The graph shows error; the yellow dashed line is zero error.
